How To Actually Use a Gua Sha for Lymphatic Drainage

Angle, pressure, and stroke direction matter. Master the gentle ritual to depuff mornings and sculpt jawlines safely.

By Sarah Lin, Holistic Skin Therapist • Published 2026-05-04

Angle, Pressure, and Stroke Direction

Gua sha is an ancient practice rooted in Traditional Chinese Medicine. When translated into facial care, gentle feather-light pressure is required to facilitate fluid movement toward lymph nodes along the clavicle and ears.

The lymphatic system does not possess its own central muscular pump like the circulatory system; it relies on smooth muscle contractions and manual movement to circulate stagnant interstitial fluid.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Holding the stone perpendicular at a 90-degree angle or dragging it across dry skin without a facial oil buffer can cause micro-capillary bruising. Always hold the stone at a flat 15-degree angle.

Ensure you apply 3 to 4 drops of a lightweight botanical oil (such as squalane or jojoba) to provide essential slip. Always stroke outwards from the center of the face, sweeping gently downward toward the clavicle terminus.
