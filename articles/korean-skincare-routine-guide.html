The 10-Step Korean Skincare Routine Explained Simply

A balanced, dermatologist-informed guide to the Korean 10-step routine: what each step does and which you actually need.

By Dr. Elena Vance, Senior Cosmetic Research Editor • Published 2026-05-14

The Real Philosophy: Prevention Over Correction

In Western skincare culture, treatments have historically leaned toward aggressive intervention: strong drying astringents, high-percentage glycolic peels, and intense exfoliants aimed at forcing skin turnover. Korean beauty takes the opposite philosophical stance: gentle, continuous hydration and barrier preservation.

Rather than stripping the epidermal mantle with harsh foaming sulfates, K-beauty emphasizes lipid restoration and cellular water retention through lightweight, layering formulations.

The 10 Steps Deconstructed

When examined through cosmetic formulation chemistry, the routine centers around four core phases: double cleansing (oil followed by a low-pH water cleanser), re-balancing pH with hydrating toners, concentrated hydration through fermented essences, and sealing the moisture barrier with ceramides.

  • Step 1 & 2: Double Cleansing: An oil-based balm breaks down sebum and waterproof sunscreen without disrupting intercellular lipids, followed by a gentle surfactant gel.
  • Step 3: Gentle Exfoliation: Polyhydroxy acids (PHAs) or mild enzymes are favored over abrasive physical scrubs.
  • Step 4: Hydrating Toner: Alcohol-free humectants applied in multiple thin layers to prep permeability.
  • Step 5: Fermented Essence: Low-molecular weight galactomyces or bifida ferment filtrates to nourish the cutaneous microbiome.
  • Step 6: Ampoules & Serums: Targeted actives addressing hyperpigmentation, elasticity, or redness.
  • Step 7: Sheet Mask: Occlusive hydration boosters utilized 1-2 times weekly.
  • Step 8: Eye Cream: Caffeine and peptide treatments formulated without fragrance for delicate periorbital tissue.
  • Step 9: Barrier Moisturizer: Physiological lipid creams (ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids) to prevent transepidermal water loss.
  • Step 10: Broad-Spectrum Sunscreen: Daily photoprotection using high-absorption organic or physical filters.

Do You Actually Need All 10 Steps?

Almost certainly not every single day. Dermatologists agree that applying too many competing actives at once increases the risk of contact dermatitis. A streamlined 4 to 5 step ritual focused on pH balance and barrier lipid recovery delivers 90% of the clinical benefits.
